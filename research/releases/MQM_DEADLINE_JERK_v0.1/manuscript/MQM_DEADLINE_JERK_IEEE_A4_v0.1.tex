\section{Exact retained fourth identity}
Write $H=H_B+K$, and introduce superoperators
\begin{equation}A=\ad_K,\quad C=\ad_{[K,H_B]},\quad D=\ad_{[[K,H_B],H_B]},\end{equation}
with $E=\ad_{[[[K,H_B],H_B],H_B]}$. Bath-only outer commutators vanish under partial trace. Right multiplication by $\ad_{H_B}$ becomes the derivation $\delta R=[R,\ad_{H_B}]$, where $\delta A=C$, $\delta C=D$, $\delta D=E$. Starting from $R_1=A$, the exact recurrence is $R_{n+1}=R_nA+\delta R_n$. Consequently,
\begin{align}
\Tr_B\ad_H^4X=\Tr_B\big(&A^4+3CA^2+2ACA+A^2C\nonumber\\&+3DA+AD+3C^2+E\big)X.\label{four}
\end{align}
The terms $3C^2$, $3DA$, $AD$ and $E$ cannot be dropped.

The source $Q\le2$ bounds are $\|K\|<14$, $\|[K,H_B]\|<256/3$, and $\|[[K,H_B],H_B]\|<4096/3$. Flat-band sixth-moment exactness at $M\ge4$ gives
\begin{equation}\|\delta^3c\|^2=\frac{128B^6}{7\pi}.
\end{equation}
The third mixed commutator has exchange norm at most $\sqrt2\|\delta^3c\|=65536/\sqrt{7\pi}<16384$. For the finite response input $\|X\|_1\le2\nu$, (\ref{four}) yields
\begin{align}
\tfrac12\|\Delta_{2,M}^{(4)}\|_1\le\nu\big(&16\cdot14^4+48\tfrac{256}{3}14^2\nonumber\\&+16\tfrac{4096}{3}14+12(\tfrac{256}{3})^2+32768\big)\nonumber\\
&=1843456\nu.\label{jfour}
\end{align}
The integer factorization is $1843456=2^8\cdot19\cdot379$, an arithmetic decomposition of this norm charge. It does not identify a geometric capacity law. The finite occupation trace $\nu$ is not a continuum photon-number bound.

\section{Separate continuum derivative gate}
No uniform real-time value bound is silently differentiated. Suppose the SAME finite and continuum retained response kernels satisfy
\begin{equation}\Delta(t)=-\int_0^t ds\int_0^s du\,F(t,s,u),
\end{equation}
with three continuous dominated derivatives. $F$ must include the final propagator, noncommuting operators, covariance and vacuum expectation; scalar filter cancellation is insufficient. Leibniz yields
\begin{align}
\Delta^{(3)}(t)={}&-(3F_t+2F_s+F_u)(t,t,t)\nonumber\\&-\int_0^t(3F_{tt}+3F_{ts}+F_{ss})(t,t,u)\,du\nonumber\\&-\int_0^t ds\int_0^s du\,F_{ttt}(t,s,u).\label{leibniz}
\end{align}
If $\epsilon_\alpha$ uniformly bounds half the trace norm of each finite-minus-continuum derivative kernel, the transfer debit is at most
\begin{equation}3\epsilon_t+2\epsilon_s+\epsilon_u+T(3\epsilon_{tt}+3\epsilon_{ts}+\epsilon_{ss})+T^2\epsilon_{ttt}/2.
\end{equation}
